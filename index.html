Sales Analytics Dashboard

Interactive dashboard tracking pipeline health, conversion rates, and revenue performance.

+

Problem: Sales leadership lacked a unified, real-time view of pipeline health — visibility was scattered across CRM exports and manual spreadsheets, making it hard to spot bottlenecks early.

Approach: Extracted opportunity data from Salesforce, structured and cleaned it in Excel, then built an interactive Power BI dashboard with slicers for time period, region, and product line — tracking lead-to-opportunity rate, win rate, deal size, and sales velocity in one live view.

Invoice Collection Optimization

Streamlined the collections process, reducing average invoice collection time by over a week.

+

Problem: Collections followed no fixed cadence — clients learned that deadlines were negotiable, disputes lingered, and the AR team spent more time chasing than reconciling.

Approach: Categorized overdue invoices by root cause (dispute, lost invoice, approval bottleneck), introduced a fixed reminder schedule at set intervals, and instituted a weekly cross-functional collections review between finance and sales.

Outcome: Average collection time dropped by 10 days, dispute resolution accelerated, and the finance team gained a repeatable process instead of reactive firefighting.

01

Why CRM Redesigns Fail (And How to Fix Them)

Focus on user adoption and data hygiene.

+

The issue I kept seeing: Organizations invest heavily in Salesforce or HubSpot, yet months later, the sales team is still using spreadsheets. The pattern was always the same — leadership imposed a new structure without understanding how the team actually worked.

How I approached it: I started by auditing the existing workflow before touching the system. I sat with the reps who lived in the CRM daily — mapping their friction points, their workarounds, their unspoken frustrations. Then I redesigned the stage structure to match how they actually sell, not how leadership wanted to report.

What I drove: A CRM that the team actually used, because it reflected their reality. Data hygiene improved naturally because the fields made sense to them. The 25% conversion lift came not from forcing adoption, but from making the tool serve the team rather than the other way around.

02

From Aviation to PropTech: Lessons Across Industries

Adapting skills across industries.

+

What I observed: Each industry I've worked in — aviation, e-commerce, healthcare, SaaS, and now PropTech — has its own language and complexity. But beneath the surface, the same operational patterns emerge: unclear processes, inconsistent follow-through, and systems that don't match how people actually work.

How I adapted: Aviation taught me composure under pressure — a VVIP escalation at check-in and a stalled six-figure deal both need a calm voice and a fast, clear next step. E-commerce taught me that when you handle thousands of tickets, you stop fearing process and start designing for it. Healthcare taught me precision — compliance errors have real consequences. And PropTech taught me that trust sells slower but lasts longer.

What I brought to each: The same systems mindset — clarity, consistency, and processes that serve people rather than control them. I built reusable frameworks that work across contexts, because the fundamentals don't change.

03

The 10-Day Collection Rule: What Actually Works

Process over pressure.

+

The problem I saw: Collections teams were stuck in a reactive cycle — chasing clients, negotiating deadlines, and reconciling manually. The AR team spent more time firefighting than preventing. Clients had learned that deadlines were negotiable.

How I diagnosed it: I mapped the end-to-end AR cycle and categorized overdue invoices by root cause — disputes, lost invoices, approval bottlenecks. The real problem wasn't difficult clients; it was unclear cadence and no structured follow-up system.

What I drove: A fixed reminder schedule that clients could anticipate, a defined escalation path that everyone understood, and reconciliation checkpoints performed on a set cadence. The result: a 10-day drop in average collection time, faster dispute resolution, and a finance team that finally had a repeatable process instead of reactive firefighting.
